## 2. Row-Level Translation Quality Issues

### KANG-TEST-00014 — **Severity A (factual error)**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `kanglish_input` | `meeting yeshtu hotte aagide?` |
| `primary_reference` | `How late has the meeting gotten?` |

**Issue:** `hotte` (ಹೊಟ್ಟೆ) means **stomach / belly / hunger** in Kannada —
not "late" or "delayed". The word for "late/delayed" is `late aagide` or `delay
aagide` (code-mixed) or `taDa aagide`. The reference translation is semantically
incorrect. The natural reading of `meeting yeshtu hotte aagide?` would be something
like "How hungry is the meeting making you?" or is simply a malformed/nonsensical
sentence. Either the input phrase is itself wrong (should be `meeting yeshtu
late aagide?`) or this row should be removed.

**Proposed action:** Replace input with `meeting yeshtu late aagide?` and update
reference to "How much has the meeting delayed?" OR remove the row entirely.
Do NOT use until a native speaker resolves this.

### KANG-TEST-00046 — **Severity A (tense contradiction)**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `kanglish_input` | `naale bittiya class?` |
| `primary_reference` | `Did you bunk class yesterday?` |

**Issue:** `naale` = tomorrow (future). The primary reference says "yesterday" —
a direct contradiction. The `disallowed_traps` column itself notes
`naale = tomorrow (tense error)` which is the very error the reference makes.
This row was generated with the intended meaning inverted: if `naale` means
tomorrow, the sentence would mean "Will you bunk class tomorrow?" not "Did you bunk
class yesterday?". Additionally, `bittiya` (from `bidu` = to leave/release) in this
context may mean "did you skip / ditch" rather than "bunk" — native confirmation needed.

**Proposed action:** Either (a) change `naale` to `ninne` (= yesterday) so the
reference matches, or (b) change the reference to "Will you skip class tomorrow?"
Do NOT use until corrected and confirmed by a native speaker.

### KANG-TEST-00053 — **Severity B (ungrammatical English reference)**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `kanglish_input` | `neevu yelli idira akka?` |
| `primary_reference` | `Where are you respectfully?` |

**Issue:** "Where are you respectfully?" is not grammatical English. The adverb
"respectfully" does not modify location queries. The intended meaning is that
`neevu` and `idira` mark formal/polite address. A natural translation would be
"Where are you (politely)?" or "Where are you, Akka?" or "Sister, where are you?"
The word `akka` (elder sister) is a direct address term and should appear in
some form in the reference.

**Proposed action:** Change primary_reference to "Akka, where are you?" and
acceptable_alternatives to "Sister, where are you?|Where are you right now, akka?"

### KANG-TEST-00060 — **Severity B (ungrammatical English reference)**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `kanglish_input` | `enu helkottilla?` |
| `primary_reference` | `What did he say not telling?` |

**Issue:** "What did he say not telling?" is not grammatical English. The Kanglish
`enu helkottilla?` most naturally parses as "What is he/she not saying?" (present
negative) or "What is (he/she) not telling (us)?". The intended subject is also
ambiguous — could be 1st, 2nd, or 3rd person depending on context.

**Proposed action:** Change primary_reference to "What is (he/she) not telling?"
and note the subject ambiguity in the notes column. Mark acceptable_alternatives
as "What isn't he telling us?|What is he keeping from us?"

### KANG-TEST-00067 — **Severity B (ungrammatical English reference)**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `kanglish_input` | `nimma class yavdu?` |
| `primary_reference` | `Which class are you politely in?` |

**Issue:** "Which class are you politely in?" is ungrammatical. The adverb "politely"
describes the speaker's register, not the location. Natural English would be
"Which class are you in?" or "What's your class/section?" The note about politeness
belongs in the notes column, not embedded in the reference.

**Proposed action:** Change primary_reference to "Which class are you in?" and add
a notes field explaining that `nimma` is the polite/plural possessive.

### KANG-TEST-00097 — **Severity B (ambiguous input, uncertain reference)**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `kanglish_input` | `avnu guru bittru` |
| `primary_reference` | `He left / he ignored bro` |

**Issue:** `guru bittru` is ambiguous. `bidu` (to let go/leave) + `avru/bittru`
could mean "He let go / released it" or colloquially "He gave up / walked away".
The sense "he ignored bro" requires `guru` to be the addressee (bro) rather than
the subject — but the input also places `avnu` (he) as subject, making `guru` a
vocative. The reference conflates two different readings without flagging the
ambiguity. Also the reference contains "/" making it technically two alternatives
packed into the primary field.

**Proposed action:** Split the slash into an acceptable_alternative. Clarify whether
this is the narrator addressing "guru (bro)" saying "he left" or "he let it go".
Mark as ambiguous and require native speaker resolution.

**Proposed action:** If the phrase is not attested in natural speech, remove the row.
If it is, a native speaker must provide the correct translation. Do not use until resolved.

## 3. Scoring Logic Issue: `going`/`coming` in Same Synonym Cluster

**File:** `scripts/benchmark.py` (copied from `scripts/score.py`)
**Issue:** The synonym cluster `{"going", "coming", "go", "come", "goes", "comes"}`
means a reference keyword "going" would also match if the model output says "coming"
— treating directional opposites as equivalent. This is wrong for sentences like
"Will you be going?" vs "Will you be coming?" which have different meaning.

**Proposed action:** Split into directional sub-clusters OR remove from SYNONYMS
entirely and rely only on stemming. This requires a change to `score.py` which is
out of scope here; the benchmark.py synonym table is fixed independently.

## 4. Contamination Check Limitations

**Issue:** `benchmark.py` has no near-duplicate detection. The current exact-string
blacklist check caught `ninne movie nodide` (fixed) but would miss:
- `ninne movie nodde` (alternate spelling)
- `naale college barthira?` (word order / case variant of the blacklisted sentence)
- `sakkath aagithe` (spelling variant of `sakkath aagide`)

**Proposed action:** The `--validate` mode in the repaired benchmark.py computes
normalized phonetic tokens and flags similarity > 0.8 against all blacklist entries.
This is advisory (manual inspection required) not automatic rejection.
